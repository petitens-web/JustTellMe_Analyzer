Welcome to JusTelMi PVT Analyzer

JusTelMi PVT Analyzer is a web-based petroleum fluid analysis platform designed to simulate, visualize, and interpret Pressure-Volume-Temperature (PVT) laboratory experiments for reservoir engineering applications. Built around the 3-parameter Soave-Redlich-Kwong (SRK3) Equation of State, this tool enables engineers to characterize reservoir fluids, simulate CCE and Differential Liberation experiments, and generate PVT property tables used in reservoir simulation software such as ECLIPSE/PVTi.

PVT analysis is fundamental to understanding how reservoir fluids behave under varying pressure and temperature conditions — knowledge that directly drives production forecasting, well deliverability calculations, and reserves estimation. This platform provides full fluid phase behavior modeling including fingerprint analysis, phase envelope generation, and saturation pressure simulation.

Explanation of Each Workflow Step

INPUT — Field and Laboratory Data

The workflow begins with collection of all field and laboratory data: reservoir pressure (4200 psia), reservoir temperature (240°F), separator conditions (120 psia / 80°F), producing GOR (1850 scf/STB), and oil API gravity (42°API). Separator gas and liquid samples are recombined to reconstruct the original reservoir fluid for laboratory analysis.

STEP 1 — Compositional Analysis

Gas chromatography is performed on the recombined fluid sample to identify and quantify each hydrocarbon component from N2 and CO2 through C10+. Mole fractions are computed and verified to sum to 100 mol%. The full composition serves as the primary input to all equation-of-state (EOS) calculations performed in subsequent steps.

STEP 2 — Fingerprint Plot

The mole percentage of each component is plotted against its carbon number to produce the fluid fingerprint. The shape of the distribution — dominated by C1 (68.5%) with a moderate C7+ fraction (~12.7%) — characterizes the fluid type and heaviness, providing a visual classification indicator before any PVT experiments are run.

STEP 3 — Differential Liberation (DL) Test

The fluid undergoes stepwise pressure depletion from 4300 psia to 15 psia at constant reservoir temperature (240°F). At each step, evolved gas and remaining oil volumes are measured, yielding solution GOR (Rs) and oil formation volume factor (Bo) at each pressure. The bubble point pressure is identified at Pb = 4250 psia where gas first evolves from the liquid phase.

STEP 4 — CCE Simulation (SRK3 EOS)

The Constant Composition Expansion experiment is simulated using the 3-parameter Soave-Redlich-Kwong (SRK3) equation of state. Relative volume (V/Vsat) is computed from 6000 psia down to 500 psia. The inflection point in the V/Vsat curve confirms Pb at 4250 psia, and the rapid expansion below Pb characterizes two-phase gas evolution behavior.

STEP 5 — Phase Envelope Generation

The complete P-T phase envelope is constructed via flash calculations across a temperature and pressure grid. Bubble point and dew point curves are traced from their convergence at the critical point. Key envelope parameters are identified: cricondenbar (maximum two-phase pressure) and cricondentherm (maximum two-phase temperature). The reservoir point (4200 psia, 240°F) is plotted to confirm its location just inside the two-phase envelope.

STEP 6 — Psat Simulation

Saturation pressure (Pb) is simulated as a function of temperature using isothermal EOS flash calculations at the reservoir fluid composition. The Pb vs. T curve is generated across a range of temperatures, and the simulated Pb at 240°F is compared against the measured laboratory value of 4250 psia to validate EOS parameter tuning before applying it to further predictions.

DECISION — Reservoir Fluid Classification

All evidence is evaluated against standard classification criteria. With GOR = 1850 scf/STB (exceeds the 1500 scf/STB threshold for volatile oil), API = 42°, Pb = 4250 psia (very close to reservoir pressure of 4200 psia), and initial Bo = 1.62 RB/STB, the fluid is conclusively classified as a Volatile Oil. This drives all subsequent production strategy decisions.

STEP 7 — PVT Property Table Compilation

All computed PVT properties — Bo, Rs, Z-factor, liquid density, gas gravity, and Bg — are compiled into a formatted table spanning the full pressure range from reservoir pressure down to stock tank conditions. The table is structured for direct import into reservoir simulation software such as ECLIPSE or PVTi for field-scale numerical simulation.

STEP 8 — Experimental vs Simulated Comparison

SRK3-simulated Bo and relative volume (V/Vsat) values are plotted alongside the experimental DL and CCE measurements. Percentage deviations are computed at each pressure step. Deviations below 0.5% confirm excellent EOS model performance, validating that the SRK3 parameters are well-tuned for this specific reservoir fluid system.

OUTPUT — Results and Interpretation

The final output comprises all generated engineering plots (fingerprint, phase envelope, CCE, DL, Psat, comparison charts), the compiled PVT property table, and the comprehensive interpretation report. The report covers fluid classification rationale, EOS performance assessment, and reservoir engineering recommendations including pressure maintenance strategy and production optimization guidance.

Results Interpretation
Comprehensive analysis of PVT simulation results
Fluid Classification

VOLATILE OIL SYSTEM

Based on the measured bubble point pressure of 4250 psia at a reservoir temperature of 240°F, a producing GOR of 1850 scf/STB, and an oil API gravity of 42°API, this reservoir fluid is classified as a volatile oil. The reservoir pressure of 4200 psia is below the bubble point pressure, indicating the reservoir exists in a two-phase region. The high initial Bo of 1.62 RB/STB and significant GOR decline with pressure are characteristic of volatile oils, which require compositional simulation for accurate recovery modeling.

EOS Performance Assessment

SRK3 MODEL ACCURACY

The 3-parameter Soave-Redlich-Kwong EOS demonstrates excellent performance for this fluid system. All Bo deviations are below 0.4%, and CCE relative volumes match within acceptable engineering tolerances (<1%). The Lohrenz-Bray-Clark viscosity correlation performs consistently across the full pressure range, yielding physically consistent density and Z-factor values. Regression convergence was confirmed with low RMS error and stable Hessian matrix diagonal dominance.

CCE Analysis

COMPRESSIBILITY & TWO-PHASE BEHAVIOR

The CCE experiment confirms a bubble point at 4250 psia. Above this pressure, single-phase liquid compressibility governs the volume behavior. Below Pb, rapid volumetric expansion occurs as gas evolves from solution. The relative volume increases from 1.0000 at Pb to approximately 7.23 at 500 psia — a 7× expansion — highlighting the importance of reservoir management strategies to control pressure above Pb and minimize two-phase flow in the reservoir pore space.

Differential Liberation Analysis

GAS LIBERATION & OIL SHRINKAGE

The differential liberation test confirms progressive gas liberation with pressure decline. Solution GOR declines from 1700 scf/STB at 4300 psia to 20 scf/STB at 15 psia. Oil formation volume factor (Bo) decreases from 1.62 RB/STB at 4300 psia to 1.03 RB/STB at stock tank conditions, representing approximately 36.4% shrinkage — significant for reserves volumetrics and material balance calculations. The measured Pb of 4250 psia closely matches the upper DL data point, confirming laboratory consistency.

Reservoir Engineering Implications

PRODUCTION STRATEGY RECOMMENDATIONS

Given the volatile oil nature of this reservoir fluid: (1) Maintaining reservoir pressure above Pb is critical to avoid two-phase flow and gas cap formation. (2) The high Bo shrinkage factor must be applied correctly when converting reservoir volumes to surface volumes. (3) Gas breakthrough risk is elevated due to volatile fluid behavior — producer spacing and drawdown management should be optimized. (4) Compositional effects should be considered in any EOS-based simulation, as the SRK3 model parameters have been validated against laboratory data for this specific fluid.
